To download historical trades for a Polymarket market in Lychee, open **Download Polymarket Trade History**, search for the market or paste its link, confirm the market details, and click **Save and Go**. Lychee loads the available history into a sheet that you can export as **CSV, Excel (XLSX), or JSON**. No coding required.

This recipe downloads public trades for **one selected market**. Personal account statements, wallet P&L, and redemption history are separate workflows.

## How to download Polymarket historical trade data

### 1. Open the recipe and find your market

Open Lychee's data picker and choose **Download Polymarket Trade History** from the **Recipes** list. You can search in plain English, type a market name, or paste a Polymarket link, market slug, or condition ID.

<RecipeFindMarketAnimation venue="polymarket" />

Try a topic such as "Fed decision" or "Presidential election 2024." If the results contain several similar questions, read the full question and closing date before selecting one.

A Polymarket event can contain several markets. For example, an election event may have a separate market for each candidate. Pasting an event link helps you find its markets; the recipe still loads trades for the specific market you select. Polymarket's [market-data overview](https://docs.polymarket.com/market-data/overview) explains that event, market, and outcome hierarchy.

You don't need to find token IDs yourself. If you want to understand the identifiers, see [how to find a Polymarket market ID, token ID, and condition ID](/guides/polymarket-market-id).

### 2. Check the market details

Selecting a result fills the **Market details** panel. Before loading the trades, check:

- **Question and slug:** confirm the contract, including any price target or date.
- **Status and outcomes:** check whether the market is open, closed, or shown as resolved, and confirm which outcomes belong to it.
- **Outcome prices and bid/ask:** inspect the displayed prices for context.
- **Volume and liquidity:** get a sense of the market's activity.
- **Opened and closes:** confirm the market's expected trading window.

Below those details, **Trade history source** shows where Lychee plans to read the trades. Source selection happens automatically.

<RecipeMarketDetailsAnimation venue="polymarket" />

There is no date picker in this recipe. It requests the market's history up to the source's available coverage and the recipe's row limit. If you need a particular day or announcement window, narrow the downloaded rows afterward.

### 3. Load the trade history

Click **Save and Go**. A progress card shows the market lookup, source selection, trade pull, and sheet placement. Wait for **Trade history ready**, then open the resulting dashboard.

<RecipeRunAnimation venue="polymarket" />

The sheet is titled **Trades · market-slug**, using the condition ID when a slug isn't available. Check the loaded row count and timestamps before starting your analysis. The recipe also creates default charts when the returned data supports them.

For an open market, the downloaded history reflects the pull you just ran. Run the recipe again when you need newer trades.

### 4. Export as CSV, Excel, or JSON

Hover over the trade sheet, open its **⋮** menu, choose **Export**, and select your format:

- **CSV:** use the table in a spreadsheet, Python, R, or another analysis tool.
- **Excel (.xlsx):** open the downloaded table directly in Excel.
- **JSON:** process the records in an application or script.

<RecipeExportAnimation venue="polymarket" />

Check the exported row count against the loaded sheet. When opening a CSV in Excel, import long token IDs and transaction hashes as text so the spreadsheet preserves them.

The public demo lets you try the recipe and inspect the data. File export requires membership; export access and data limits depend on your [Lychee plan](/#pricing).

## What is in the downloaded trade data?

The columns depend on whether the recipe uses Polymarket Live, Polymarket Historical, or both. Start with these fields:

- **Outcome:** the outcome whose shares changed hands, such as Yes or No.
- **Side:** the buy or sell direction recorded for the execution. Historical fill rows calculate this from the taker's perspective.
- **Price:** the execution price per share. A value of **0.39** means 39 cents per share.
- **Size:** the number of shares exchanged.
- **Timestamp:** when the execution occurred. Live-format sheets also include a readable execution-time field.
- **Transaction hash:** an identifier that helps you trace the on-chain transaction.

Polymarket's [trade-data documentation](https://docs.polymarket.com/market-data/public-analytics) describes trade price in USDC per share and size in shares. For example, 100 shares at 0.39 represent 39 USDC of execution value before fees.

<PolymarketTradeRowAnatomy />

Keep **side** and **outcome** separate. Buying No shares is different from selling Yes shares, even when the two actions express a similar view of the question.

### Historical fills and Live trade rows

Polymarket Historical stores individual exchange fills and includes maker/taker details and amounts. Lychee derives readable outcome, side, price, size, and USDC fields from those records. Fills that cannot be interpreted as a USDC-for-shares exchange can have blank derived fields.

For a market whose history spans both sources, Lychee groups older fills sharing a transaction, taker, outcome, and side into a Live-style row. It calculates the grouped price from the total USDC exchanged divided by the total shares.

<PolymarketFillsToTradeRowDiagram />

That means historical fill counts and combined trade-row counts can differ. A transaction hash can also appear on multiple rows. Check the source and row structure before comparing counts or removing apparent duplicates.

## Where does the historical trade data come from?

The recipe uses a **January 1, 2026 UTC** boundary between its historical archive and newer trade data:

- **Markets opened on or after the boundary:** read from Polymarket Live.
- **Markets closed before the boundary:** read from Polymarket Historical first, with a Live fallback if the archive returns no rows.
- **Markets spanning the boundary:** combine newer Live trades with older Historical fills. If there are no usable older fills, the recipe tries Live for the history instead.

Here, **Live** is the source name. A pull from that source can include past executions.

<PolymarketSourceTimelineDiagram />

For broader queries across historical markets, prices, events, and resolutions, use the [Polymarket historical-data workspace](/polymarket-historical-data). This recipe is the shortcut for a single market's trade download.

## Check coverage and narrow your date range

One sheet holds up to **500,000 rows**. A large market can exceed the pull limit, so don't assume a successful run contains its entire lifetime. Compare the earliest and latest execution timestamps with the period you need, and review any limit message on the progress card.

Opening and closing dates are market metadata. They do not prove that trades exist at either endpoint or that every execution between them was returned.

To study a smaller window, export the sheet, sort by execution time, and retain the rows inside your chosen period. For example, keep the hour around a Fed announcement or the final day before an election market closed. Use one time zone consistently and record the window in your research notes.

For combined-source histories, check row granularity as well as dates before aggregating activity across the source boundary.

## Trade history, price history, and order books

Trade history records executions. Price history is a series of price observations, and candles summarize activity over intervals. Order books show bids and asks available at a particular moment.

This recipe exports execution data. It does not reconstruct the historical order book or show all the orders that were available before each trade. A historical execution price alone cannot establish whether your own order would have filled at that price.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I download Polymarket historical data without coding?

Yes. For a selected market's trade data, open the recipe, find the market, confirm its details, and click **Save and Go**. Export the resulting sheet in the format you need.

### Can I export Polymarket trades as CSV or Excel?

Yes. Open the sheet's **⋮** menu, choose **Export**, then select CSV or Excel (.xlsx). JSON is also available. File export requires membership and is subject to your plan's limits.

### Can I download trades from closed or resolved markets?

The recipe can search closed markets and select a historical source for them. Available rows depend on source coverage and the pull limit. Check the returned timestamps against your research window.

### Do I need a market ID or token ID?

No. Search by name or paste a Polymarket link. If you already have a market slug or condition ID, you can use that instead.

### Can I filter the download by date?

There is no date filter in the recipe. It requests the market history within its coverage and row limit. Filter the exported timestamps afterward for a smaller window.

### Does this export my personal Polymarket transactions?

This recipe selects a market rather than an account. It does not produce your personal account statement, wallet P&L report, or redemption history, and it is not a Polymarket US account-export workflow.

### Does it download every market in an event?

The recipe downloads one selected market. An event link can return several market choices; choose the specific question you want to study.
