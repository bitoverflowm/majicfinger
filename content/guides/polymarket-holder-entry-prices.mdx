YES was priced at **30.5¢** when we opened a Polymarket contract on three consecutive Fed rate hikes. The people still holding YES had paid **24.54¢ per share on average**. Most of those shares belonged to one wallet. Open its trade history and you can see how much of the chart comes down to a single buyer. ## What did holders on each side pay?

The contract asked whether the Fed would hike rates at all three decisions in December, January, and March. At **14:11 UTC on November 17, 2026**, Lychee returned 12 YES holder positions and 20 NO holder positions.

- **YES averaged 24.54¢ across 3,664.37 shares.** Every YES position had a recorded entry. At the snapshot price of 30.5¢, the shares were worth about 6¢ more apiece than their average entry.
- **NO averaged 69.58¢ across 2,848.18 shares with a recorded entry.** NO was priced at 69.5¢, putting that group within a tenth of a cent of its average cost. Another **816.18 NO shares** had no recorded entry price.

<YesNoEntrySidesAnimation />

Those missing entries change the story. If you count the 816.18 shares as though someone paid zero for them, the NO average falls to **54.08¢**. That would make NO holders look comfortably ahead. We can establish that the holders with recorded costs were near break-even. What the remaining shares cost is unknown.

## Where did most holders get in?

In **Where the supply was bought**, select **Shares**. Each bar groups current shares by their holder’s average entry in one-cent intervals. A tall bar can come from one large wallet.

On YES, **82.2% of shares** fell in the **23–24¢** band. Every share in that bar belonged to **puravida**. On NO, **65.1% of shares with a known entry** fell in the **67–68¢** band. Knowing who owns a bar changes how you read it: that big YES bar tells you a great deal about one buyer and very little about where the other eleven holders entered.

## How to check holder entry prices in another market

1. **Choose the exact contract.** An event can contain several questions with different deadlines or outcomes. Open the one whose YES and NO prices you want to compare. [Search a Polymarket market in Holder Terminal](/polymarket-holder-terminal)
2. **Read each side against its own price.** In **Profit, loss and ownership**, compare YES entry with current YES, then NO entry with current NO. Check the histogram’s missing-entry note. The overview’s average can include shares with a zero entry field. For a known-cost average, multiply each recorded entry by the shares still held, add those values, and divide by the shares with recorded entries.
3. **Follow the tallest bar to the holders table.** Find the wallets whose average entries sit in that band. Open a large holder and check its buys and sells in the same market. If one wallet dominates a band, the next question is how much that holding matters to the wallet itself.
