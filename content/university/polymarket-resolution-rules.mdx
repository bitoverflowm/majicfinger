# The Headline Says It Happened. Why Did Your YES Lose?

**Lychee University · Lesson 10**

Northbridge scores 102 points. You bought YES on “Will Northbridge score at least 100?” Time to celebrate?

Read the rules first. In this hypothetical contract, only regulation-time points count. Northbridge had 98 before overtime.

<HeadlineVsRulesAnimation />

**Polymarket resolution rules define the specific outcome being settled.** A headline can accurately describe what happened while leaving out a condition your contract requires. The title gets you to the question; the rules tell you what counts.

## What should you look for in resolution rules?

Polymarket's documentation identifies the resolution source, end date, and treatment of edge cases as core parts of a market's rules. It explicitly tells traders to read beyond the title. [Polymarket's resolution documentation](https://docs.polymarket.com/concepts/resolution)

Turn the rules into a short checklist:

| Check | Question to answer |
| --- | --- |
| **Condition** | What exactly must happen for YES? |
| **Source** | Which record, announcement, or data provider determines it? |
| **Time** | Which deadline, time zone, or observation window applies? |
| **Exceptions** | What happens after a postponement, correction, tie, or ambiguity? |

<ResolutionChecklistAnimation />

The examples relevant to each checklist item depend on the particular market. Don't assume that a condition from one contract carries into the next.

## Can two similar questions have different outcomes?

Return to our invented basketball game:

| Hypothetical contract | Counted score | Result |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| At least 100 points, regulation only | 98 | NO |
| At least 100 points, including overtime | 102 | YES |

<TwoContractsSameGameExplorer />

Both can resolve consistently with their rules. Neither headline score alone tells you which contract you own.

The same distinction can arise when markets use different official sources, reporting windows, or definitions. Before treating a price difference across venues as an opportunity, check whether the contracts promise the same payout under the same circumstances.

## Who decides how Polymarket resolves?

The process depends on the market type.

Polymarket documents UMA's proposal-and-dispute process for its UMA-resolved markets. It separately describes Chainlink TWAP resolution for Up/Down markets, where specified reference-price observations determine the result.

<WhoResolvesExplorer />

That distinction matters: “Polymarket uses UMA” is not a complete explanation for every contract. Start with the market's own source and rules. A full explanation of oracle governance is a separate lesson.

## Does the event ending mean I get paid immediately?

The end of the real-world event, the end of trading, final resolution, and redemption are different steps. An outcome may still need confirmation or a dispute may delay finalization.

<ResolutionPipelineAnimation />

Polymarket's [resolution guide](https://docs.polymarket.com/concepts/resolution) describes those mechanisms and how winning tokens become redeemable. Check the actual market status rather than treating a news alert as proof that settlement is complete.

## What if the wording is ambiguous?

Look for published additional context and the platform's clarification or dispute process. Don't substitute your preferred interpretation for a settled answer. Polymarket documents that clarifications can be issued after trading begins, subject to limits on changing the question's fundamental intent.

<AmbiguousWordingAnimation />

Keep the rules and observation date with your research notes. When explaining a disputed result, distinguish what the contract said, what evidence was available, and what the resolution process decided.

If you're comparing venues, [Lychee's Kalshi–Polymarket comparison guide](/guides/how-to-compare-kalshi-polymarket-odds) provides a starting point for researching matching questions. Read each contract's rules before calling them equivalent.

Before buying YES, finish this sentence in plain English:

<ResolutionSentenceExplorer />

**“I get the winning payout if this exact condition is met, according to this source, within this window.”**
