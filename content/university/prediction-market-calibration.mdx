# A 90% Prediction Lost. Was It Wrong?

**Lychee University · Lesson 9**

A market gives an event a 90% chance. The event doesn't happen. Someone posts the screenshot: “Prediction markets got it wrong again.”

The favored outcome lost. But a 90% forecast left room for that result.

<NinetyPercentLossAnimation />

**Calibration asks whether events assigned a given probability happen at roughly that frequency across many forecasts.** If forecasts are well calibrated, events assigned 90% should happen about nine times in ten over a sufficiently large, relevant set. It doesn't tell you which tenth will fail.

## What does a calibrated prediction market look like?

Imagine a hypothetical collection of binary markets. We record each forecast exactly one day before its event and later check the resolved outcome.

| Recorded probability | Number of forecasts | Events that happened |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 50% | 100 | 50 |
| 70% | 100 | 70 |
| 90% | 100 | 90 |

<CalibrationBucketsAnimation />

The probabilities match the observed frequencies in this constructed example. Among the 90% forecasts, ten events failed to occur. Those losses are compatible with the group's calibration.

Drag the hit count for a hundred 90% forecasts on the toy reliability plot. Real data won't usually sit so neatly on the diagonal.

<ReliabilityPlotExplorer />

Sampling variation matters, especially with few forecasts. Ninety successes out of 100 is a useful observation, not proof that the forecasting process is perfectly calibrated. [Metaculus explains calibration in terms of probabilities matching observed frequencies.](https://www.metaculus.com/labor-hub/)

## Can one result prove a forecast was good or bad?

One result gives you information, but cannot establish calibration.

A 99% forecast that fails deserves scrutiny: it expressed much more confidence than a 60% forecast. Yet neither one alone tells you whether a forecaster systematically overstates probabilities. You need the record, including ordinary successes and other failures.

<OneResultVsRecordExplorer />

The same applies in reverse. One successful 1% prediction doesn't establish exceptional forecasting skill. Highlighting only the most surprising win or loss selects the story after seeing the ending.

## How is calibration different from picking winners?

Imagine a forecaster who assigns 51% to a hundred outcomes and gets all hundred right. It picked every winner, yet its 51% forecasts occurred far more often than stated.

Another forecaster assigns 90% to a hundred outcomes and gets ninety right. It picks fewer winners, but its probabilities match the observed frequency in this example.

<WinnersVsCalibrationExplorer />

A forecast can therefore have an impressive winner-picking record while describing uncertainty poorly. Calibration evaluates the probability attached to the prediction.

## What should you check before comparing markets?

Start with **the forecast time**. A price recorded a month before an election answers a harder question than one recorded after most votes have been counted. Kalshi's calibration research explicitly examines time to resolution rather than treating every timestamp as equivalent. [Read Kalshi's study summary.](https://kalshi.com/research/publications/calibration)

<ForecastTimingAnimation />

Then check the sample. Keep the market category, probability bands, number of observations, and treatment of related contracts visible. A hundred contracts tied to one election are not a hundred unrelated elections. Broad averages can also conceal differences between categories.

<SampleCompositionExplorer />

For real prices, group nearby probabilities into stated ranges and report how many observations fall in each. Leave room for uncertainty rather than treating every deviation from the diagonal as a discovery.

## How can you investigate it yourself?

Keep a dated record of the question, forecast probability, observation time, resolution criteria, and eventual result. Decide those rules before selecting examples.

<CalibrationRecordAnimation />

For a deeper research application, explore [Lychee's Kalshi political-market calibration study](/guides/kalshi-political-prediction-market-accuracy).

The next time a 90% favorite loses, ask a better follow-up: **what happened across the other forecasts made with the same confidence?**
